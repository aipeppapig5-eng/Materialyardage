Gravel Driveway Guide: Depth, Volume and Ordering

Gravel driveways are often built in layers. Quantity depends on driveway area, the depth of each layer and aggregate density.

Measure the driveway

Multiply length by width for a rectangular driveway. For curves or widening sections, divide the driveway into simple shapes and add their areas.

Cubic feet = area (ft²) × depth (in) ÷ 12
Cubic yards = cubic feet ÷ 27

Plan each layer separately

Sub-base, intermediate aggregate and surface gravel can have different functions. Estimate each layer independently rather than treating the whole driveway as one material.

For heavy traffic, weak subgrade or drainage-sensitive sites, follow the project specification or local engineering guidance for layer thickness and material type.

Convert volume to tons

Multiply cubic yards by a density expressed in tons per cubic yard. Moisture, grading and compaction change delivered weight, so supplier-specific density is preferable.

Estimate gravel by layer, not just by driveway footprint

Driveway designs may use a compacted sub-base and a separate wearing course. Their materials and target thicknesses can differ, so calculate each layer separately and add the volumes only after both estimates are complete. A surface-depth rule of thumb is not a substitute for local soil, drainage, frost and traffic requirements.

Worked example

A 40 ft × 12 ft driveway has an area of 480 ft². A nominal 4-inch layer is 480 × (4 ÷ 12) = 160 ft³, or 160 ÷ 27 = 5.93 yd³ before compaction-related adjustments and waste. A second 2-inch layer over the same footprint is 2.96 yd³ before adjustments. Do not automatically apply the same density to different products.

Use the Gravel Calculator and consult the material density chart for rough weight planning.

Driveway ordering checklist

  • Confirm whether dimensions refer to the full area or only the section receiving new gravel.
  • Ask whether the specified thickness is loose or compacted thickness.
  • Get separate product names and density/yield information for each layer.
  • Plan drainage and finished levels before calculating final quantities.
  • Check truck payload and delivery minimums with the supplier; estimated tons are not certified load weights.
