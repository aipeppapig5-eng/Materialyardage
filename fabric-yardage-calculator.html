How the fabric yardage estimate works

The calculator determines how many pieces fit across the usable fabric width. It then calculates the number of rows required and multiplies the rows by the cut length plus your extra length allowance. Inches are converted to yards by dividing by 36.

Worked example

Suppose you need four pieces, each 36 inches long by 20 inches wide, from 44-inch-wide fabric. Two pieces fit across the width, so two rows are required. With 2 inches of extra length per row, the estimate is 2 × 38 = 76 inches, or about 2.11 yards. Consider buying extra for shrinkage, directional prints, matching patterns, defects and layout constraints.

Important assumptions and limitations

  • Use the usable width, not the full selvage-to-selvage width if the selvages cannot be used.
  • Check grain direction, nap, print direction, pattern repeat and the cutting layout before buying fabric.
  • Garment patterns may require different sizes, mirrored pieces, facings, linings or extra fabric for matching stripes and plaids.
  • Prewash allowance and fabric shrinkage vary by fiber and finish; follow the fabric and pattern instructions.
  • This is a basic yardage estimator, not a substitute for a pattern maker's cutting layout.

Frequently asked questions

How many yards are in one yard of fabric?

One yard is 36 inches (3 feet). The calculator converts the total cutting length in inches into yards.

Does fabric width affect yardage?

Yes. Wider fabric may allow more pieces to fit across each row, reducing the total length needed. Use the actual usable width of the fabric you plan to buy.

How much extra fabric should I buy?

There is no universal allowance. Add extra for hems, pattern matching, shrinkage, directional prints, mistakes and any special cutting instructions. Follow your sewing pattern's recommendation where available.
