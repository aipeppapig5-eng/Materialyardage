Brick Wall Calculator Guide

This estimator converts wall face area into an approximate brick count using a selected brick profile and an optional cutting and waste allowance.

How the estimate works

Wall area is calculated from length × height. The selected profile supplies an approximate bricks-per-square-foot factor. The waste allowance is then applied and the result is rounded up to a whole brick.

Important assumptions

  • Brick dimensions and mortar joints vary by region and manufacturer.
  • Openings such as doors and windows should be subtracted from the wall area for a more precise order.
  • Actual waste depends on cuts, breakage, bond pattern and site handling.

Frequently asked questions

Does this include mortar?

No. Use the mortar calculator separately when you need an estimate of joint mortar volume.

Should openings be removed?

Yes. For a more accurate order, subtract the area of doors, windows and other large openings before applying the brick factor.

Why is the result rounded up?

Bricks are normally purchased as whole units, and a small surplus is preferable to a shortage during installation.

For the calculation assumptions used on this site, see the MaterialYardage methodology.

How brick quantity is estimated

The basic method divides wall area by the face area of one brick and then applies an allowance for cuts, breakage and layout. Mortar joints affect the effective module size, so the calculator's assumptions should be checked against the actual brick and joint specification.

Worked example

Wall area and openings

For a 20 ft × 8 ft wall, the gross area is 160 ft². If a 3 ft × 7 ft door is present, subtract 21 ft² before estimating brick quantities. Add an appropriate project allowance after the net wall area is calculated.

Before ordering

  • Confirm the nominal brick dimensions with the supplier.
  • Measure openings and subtract them from gross wall area.
  • Ask for the supplier's bricks-per-square-foot coverage when available.
  • Order matching batches where colour and appearance matter.

Why counts vary

Brick sizes, joint thickness, bond pattern, corners, returns and waste can all change the actual count. Use the calculator for preliminary planning and the project specification for final procurement.
