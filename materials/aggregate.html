FAQ

Why can supplier tonnage differ?

Moisture, grading, compaction and quarry source affect bulk density. Use supplier documentation when available.

Should I add an allowance?

An allowance can cover depth variation, spillage and measuring uncertainty. The appropriate percentage depends on the project.

Aggregate quantity basics

Aggregate volume is calculated from the project dimensions and layer depth. Approximate weight is then derived from a bulk-density assumption. Crushed stone, gravel and other aggregates can have different densities even when their volumes are identical.

Worked example

Driveway base layer

For a 40 ft × 10 ft area at 4 inches, the geometric requirement is about 4.94 cubic yards. The weight estimate depends on the selected aggregate's bulk density and should be checked against the supplier's product data.

Compaction matters

Base materials may compact after placement. If the finished thickness is specified after compaction, the ordering allowance should account for the material and installation method rather than simply adding an arbitrary percentage.

Before ordering

  • Identify the exact aggregate type and grading.
  • Confirm whether the supplier's density is loose or compacted.
  • Ask for coverage or tonnage guidance for the intended application.
  • Separate structural base quantities from decorative stone quantities.
