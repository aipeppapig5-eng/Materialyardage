Compost & Soil Mix Calculator Guide

This calculator estimates the component volumes in a soil or compost blend from a target volume and selected proportions.

How the estimate works

The target volume is divided according to the entered blend percentages. Component totals should add to 100 percent before ordering. Final material behavior varies with moisture, particle size and product composition.

Important assumptions

  • Percentages should total 100 percent for a complete blend.
  • Bulk densities vary substantially between compost, topsoil and amendments.
  • Use supplier product specifications when weight-based ordering is required.

Frequently asked questions

Can I enter any blend ratio?

Yes, provided the component percentages form a complete blend and match your intended recipe.

Does volume equal weight?

No. Different materials have different bulk densities, so equal volumes can have very different weights.

Will compost shrink after installation?

Organic materials can settle and decompose over time. Consider the intended finished depth when deciding whether an allowance is appropriate.

For the calculation assumptions used on this site, see the MaterialYardage methodology.

What the estimate means

The calculator first determines the total fill volume from bed length, width and depth, then applies the selected soil and compost proportions. The result is a volume estimate; the displayed weight is only an approximation because compost and topsoil bulk density changes with moisture, screening and organic content.

Worked example

12 ft × 4 ft bed at 12 in depth

The geometric volume is 48 cubic feet, or about 1.78 cubic yards. With a 10% planning allowance, the order quantity becomes about 1.96 cubic yards. At a 70/30 blend, that is roughly 1.37 yd³ of topsoil and 0.59 yd³ of compost.

Planning and ordering tips

  • Measure the finished internal bed dimensions rather than the outside frame when the walls are thick.
  • Use the supplier's stated bag volume when converting bulk volume to bags.
  • Do not assume compost and soil have the same weight per cubic yard.
  • For settling-prone organic material, discuss the appropriate allowance with the supplier or installer.

Common mistakes

  • Using the outside dimensions of a raised bed without accounting for wall thickness.
  • Confusing a 70/30 volume ratio with a weight ratio.
  • Using a generic density to place a weight-based order when the supplier publishes its own value.
