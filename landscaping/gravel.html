Gravel & Road Base Calculator Guide

This estimator helps convert aggregate dimensions into cubic yards and approximate weight for gravel, crushed stone and road-base materials.

How the estimate works

Calculate rectangular volume first, then multiply cubic yards by the selected bulk-density assumption to estimate tons. Density is approximate because aggregate moisture, grading and compaction affect delivered weight.

Important assumptions

  • Use the supplier's density or delivered weight information when available.
  • Driveways and compacted base courses may require a design-specific thickness and compaction allowance.
  • Do not assume all gravel products have the same tons-per-yard value.

Frequently asked questions

How accurate is the tonnage?

It is an estimate based on an assumed bulk density. Actual delivered weight can differ.

Should I add waste?

A modest ordering allowance can cover grading, uneven subgrade and handling losses. Compacted base work may need a separate compaction consideration.

Can this calculate a driveway?

Yes for a preliminary rectangular estimate. Split irregular driveways into sections for better accuracy.

For the calculation assumptions used on this site, see the MaterialYardage methodology.

What the estimate means

The calculator converts the measured project area and layer depth into cubic volume, then uses an approximate bulk-density assumption to estimate weight. Aggregate weight is not fixed: moisture, stone size, grading and compaction all affect the delivered tonnage.

Worked example

40 ft × 10 ft area at 4 in depth

The geometric requirement is about 133.3 cubic feet, or 4.94 cubic yards. At an assumed 1.4 tons per cubic yard, the unadjusted weight is about 6.91 tons. A supplier may quote a different density, so confirm the product specification before ordering by weight.

Base versus decorative gravel

Road base, crusher run and compactable aggregate are usually selected for structural layers, while decorative stone is commonly specified for appearance and drainage. The same volume can produce different weights and coverage depending on the material.

Ordering tips

  • State the intended layer depth when requesting a supplier quote.
  • Allow for compaction where the material is installed as a structural base.
  • Check whether the supplier sells by cubic yard, ton, tonne, or truckload.
  • Use a measured test area when a precise finished depth matters.
