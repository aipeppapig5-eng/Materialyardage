Gravel calculation formula

Cubic feet = length × width × (depth ÷ 12) Cubic yards = cubic feet ÷ 27 Estimated tons = cubic yards × assumed bulk density

For this calculator, the default gravel density is approximately 1.40 tons per cubic yard. Crushed stone, pea gravel, river rock and road base can have different bulk densities.

Worked example

A 40 ft × 10 ft area at 4 inches deep is about 133.33 cubic feet, or 4.94 cubic yards. At 1.40 tons per cubic yard, the estimated weight is about 6.91 tons.

How deep should gravel be?

Depth depends on the application. Walkways, decorative beds and vehicle areas can require different construction depths. For driveways and traffic-bearing surfaces, consider the sub-base, drainage and local construction practice rather than relying on a single generic depth.

Gravel vs. crushed stone

Gravel is a broad category. Rounded stone, angular crushed stone and graded aggregate behave differently during placement and compaction. Ask the supplier for the product's coverage and bulk density when ordering.

Ordering allowance

Measurements of irregular areas are approximate. Consider a reasonable allowance for variation and compaction, then confirm the final quantity with the supplier.

FAQ

How many tons are in a cubic yard of gravel?

A common estimating figure is around 1.4 tons per cubic yard, but actual weight varies by material, moisture and grading.

Should I order exactly the calculated amount?

Not necessarily. The calculator provides an estimate. Site conditions, compaction and supplier measurement practices can change the final amount required.
